The Frog King
In olden times when wishing still helped one, there lived a king whose daughters were all beautiful.
The youngest was so beautiful that the sun itself, which has seen so much, was astonished whenever it shone in her face.
Near the castle of this king there lay a great dark forest, and under an old lime-tree in the forest was a well.
And when the day was very warm, the king's child went out into the forest and sat down by the side of the cool fountain.

The Wolf and the Seven Young Kids
There was once an old goat who had seven little ones, and was as fond of them as ever mother was of her children.
One day she had to go into the wood to fetch food for them, so she called them all round her.
Dear children, said she, I am going out into the wood; be on your guard against the wolf.
If he comes in, he will eat you all up, skin, hair, and everything.
The wolf will often disguise himself, but you will know him at once by his rough voice and his black feet.

The Bremen Town Musicians
A man had a donkey, which had carried the corn-sacks to the mill indefatigably for many a long year.
But his strength was going, and he was growing more and more unfit for work.
The donkey saw that he had not long to live, and he ran away.
He set out on the road to Bremen, where he thought he could become town-musician.
Soon he met a dog lying panting on the road, as if he had run till he was tired.

The Fisherman and His Wife
There was once a fisherman who lived with his wife in a filthy hovel near the sea.
He went to fish every day, and he fished, and he fished.
And at last one day, as he was sitting looking deep down into the shining water, his hook went down to the bottom.
When he drew it up again he had caught a great flounder.
Then the flounder said to him, Hark you, fisherman, I pray you, let me live; I am not a real flounder, I am an enchanted prince.

Rapunzel
There were once a man and a woman who had long in vain wished for a child.
At last the woman hoped that God was about to grant her desire.
These people had a little window at the back of their house from which a splendid garden could be seen.
The garden was full of the most beautiful flowers and herbs.
But there was a high wall round about it, and no one ventured to go into it.

Snow White
It was in the middle of winter, and the snow-flakes were falling like feathers from the sky.
A queen sat at a window sewing, and the frame of the window was made of black ebony.
And as she was sewing and looking out across the snow, she pricked her finger with the needle.
Three drops of blood fell upon the snow, and the red looked pretty upon the white snow.

The Golden Goose
There was a man who had three sons, the youngest of whom was called Dummling.
Dummling was despised and mocked by the rest of the family.
On a day, the eldest son was going into the forest to hew wood, and his mother gave him a fine pancake and a bottle of wine.
The four winds of heaven successfully blew off their hats, and the little fellows were given a feast by Dummling.
The Golden Goose grew so wonderfully heavy that each of them was forced to carry it.

The Twelve Dancing Princesses
There was a king who had twelve beautiful daughters.
They slept in twelve beds all in one room, and when they went to bed, the doors were shut and locked up.
But every morning her shoes were found to be quite worn through as if they had been danced in all night.
The king could not discover how it happened, so he gave notice that whoever could discover where the princesses danced would marry one of them.

Rumpelstiltskin
By the side of a wood, in a country a great way off, ran a fine stream of water.
And upon the stream there stood a mill, and it happened that the miller fell into a sudden fancy.
A miller had a pretty daughter, full of love for her father.
One day she had to fetch some water, and she met a strange little man who demanded her first-born child in return for his aid.
And the little man danced round and round, and at last he cried, Rumpelstiltskin is my name.

The Elves and the Shoemaker
A shoemaker, by no fault of his own, had become so poor that at last he had nothing left but leather for one pair of shoes.
At evening he cut the shoes out ready to work upon next morning.
When he got up in the morning, ready to work, he found the shoes finished and standing upon his table.
He did not know what to say, or what to think, for the shoes were made most beautifully.
